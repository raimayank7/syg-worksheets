About this worksheet

Four kinds of fraction practice. Add and Subtract use fractions with the same denominator, so only the numerators change. Simplify asks for the lowest form, for example 6/8 = 3/4. Compare puts two fractions side by side, often with different denominators.

What this skill is

A fraction names equal parts of a whole. The bottom number, the denominator, tells into how many equal parts the whole is cut. The top number, the numerator, tells how many of those parts are taken. A fraction can be written in many ways without changing its size: 1/2, 2/4 and 5/10 are the same amount. The simplest form uses the smallest numbers.

A worked example

Add 5/8 + 7/8.

  1. The denominators are the same, so add the numerators: 5 + 7 = 12. The sum is 12/8.
  2. Simplify: 4 divides both 12 and 8, giving 3/2.
  3. 3/2 is more than a whole. As a mixed number it is 1 1/2.

Compare 3/4 and 5/7. Cross multiply: 3 × 7 = 21 and 4 × 5 = 20. Since 21 is more than 20, 3/4 is the bigger fraction.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Same denominator: add or subtract the top numbers and keep the bottom number. The size of the pieces does not change.
  • To simplify, divide the top and the bottom by the same number until no number divides both.
  • To compare fractions with different denominators, cross multiply, or change both to the same denominator.
  • The answer key gives answers in simplest form, with improper fractions also shown as mixed numbers.

How to use this sheet at home and in class

  • At home: fold a strip of paper into 8 equal parts and shade 5, then 7 more on a second strip. The child sees why the answer passes one whole.
  • In class: teach the four types on different days. "Simplify" should come before "Compare", because comparing often ends with simplifying.
  • Ask for answers in simplest form from the first day. The answer page shows them that way, so marking stays easy.

Mistakes to watch for

  • Adding the denominators as well: 5/8 + 7/8 = 12/16. The size of the parts, eighths, does not change when you put parts together.
  • Stopping halfway when simplifying: 12/8 becomes 6/4 and is left there. Keep dividing until only 1 divides both numbers.
  • Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction. 1/8 is smaller than 1/4, because the whole is cut into more pieces.

Common questions

What are like fractions?

Fractions with the same denominator, such as 2/7 and 4/7. They can be added or subtracted directly.
