About this worksheet

Sums about money make arithmetic real. These sheets use rupees: adding prices, adding amounts with paise written as decimals (₹12.50), and finding the change after paying with a note.

What this skill is

Money sums are ordinary addition and subtraction, but with a meaning the child cares about. Rupees and paise also give a gentle first meeting with decimals: 100 paise make one rupee, so ₹12.50 is twelve rupees and fifty paise. Finding the change is subtraction that many shopkeepers do by counting up.

A worked example

A notebook costs ₹64.50 and a pen costs ₹23.75. You pay with a ₹100 note.

  1. Add the prices with the points in a line: 64.50 + 23.75 = 88.25.
  2. Change by counting up from ₹88.25: 75 paise takes you to ₹89, one rupee more to ₹90, and ₹10 more to ₹100.
  3. 0.75 + 1 + 10 = 11.75.

The bill is ₹88.25 and the change is ₹11.75. Check: 88.25 + 11.75 = 100.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Play shop at home with real coins and notes before using the sheet.
  • For change, teach counting up: from ₹64 to ₹70 is 6, from 70 to 100 is 30, so the change is ₹36.
  • Rupees and paise: keep the points in a line, as in any decimal sum. 50 paise is half a rupee.

How to use this sheet at home and in class

  • At home: let the child pay at the shop for one small thing and count the change. Then do "Work out the change" on paper the same evening.
  • In class: set up a pretend shop with price tags and paper notes. The sheet becomes the bill book.
  • Take the types in order: two amounts, three amounts, rupees and paise, then change. Each one adds a single new difficulty.

Mistakes to watch for

  • Writing 5 paise as ₹0.5. Five paise is ₹0.05; ₹0.5 would be fifty paise.
  • Points not kept in a line when the amounts have different lengths, such as ₹7.50 + ₹112.25.
  • Taking the smaller digit from the bigger when working out change from 100. Counting up avoids borrowing across the zeros altogether.
