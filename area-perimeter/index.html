About this worksheet

Perimeter is the distance around a shape; area is the surface inside it. For a rectangle, perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth) and area = length × breadth. For a square, perimeter = 4 × side and area = side × side.

What this skill is

Perimeter and area measure two different things about the same shape. Perimeter is a length: how far you walk going once around the edge. Area is a surface: how many unit squares cover the inside. A fence, a border and a photo frame need perimeter. Paint, tiles and carpet need area.

A worked example

A rectangle is 12 cm long and 7 cm broad.

  1. Perimeter: add length and breadth, 12 + 7 = 19, then double it, because there are two of each side: 2 × 19 = 38 cm.
  2. Area: multiply length by breadth: 12 × 7 = 84 square cm.

For a square of side 9 cm: perimeter = 4 × 9 = 36 cm, and area = 9 × 9 = 81 square cm.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Ask the child to draw a rough figure and write the measures on it before using a formula.
  • Units matter: perimeter is in cm or m, area is in square cm or square m.
  • Measure a real book or table top and find both. It makes the difference between the two clear.

How to use this sheet at home and in class

  • At home: measure the top of a table or a notebook with a ruler and find both. Then ask which answer you would need to put a ribbon around it and which to cover it with paper.
  • In class: on squared paper, let children draw two different rectangles with a perimeter of 20 squares and count the area of each. They find that the same perimeter can hold different areas.
  • Begin with "Small numbers" so the tables do not get in the way of the idea.

Mistakes to watch for

  • Mixing the two formulas: multiplying for perimeter or adding for area.
  • Adding only one length and one breadth for the perimeter and forgetting to double.
  • Leaving out the unit, or writing cm for area. Area is always in square units.
