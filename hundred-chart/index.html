About this worksheet

A ten by ten grid of numbers with some left out. Filling it teaches the order of numbers and the pattern behind them: going one square right adds 1, going one square down adds 10.

What this skill is

A hundred chart sets out a hundred numbers in ten rows of ten. Because each row holds exactly ten numbers, the chart shows the tens and ones of our number system as a picture: every number in a column ends in the same digit, and every number in a row, except the last, starts with the same tens digit.

A worked example

Find 47 on a full chart from 1 to 100. It is in the fifth row, seventh square.

  1. One square to the right is 48: one more.
  2. One square to the left is 46: one less.
  3. One square down is 57: ten more.
  4. One square up is 37: ten less.

So a blank square can be filled from any neighbour. If the square above a blank is 62, the blank is 72. This is also how to add 10, or 20, without counting.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Choose "None" to print a full chart for the wall or for skip counting with colour pencils.
  • Ask pattern questions on a full chart: colour every fifth number; what do you see?
  • An empty grid ("All") is a good test at the end of the term.

How to use this sheet at home and in class

  • At home: start with "A few" blanks, then "About half". Print one full chart and keep it; it is useful for months.
  • In class: on a full chart, let children colour the numbers they say when counting by 2, by 5 and by 10, each in a different colour, and talk about the stripes that appear.
  • For classes 2 and 3, choose a chart that starts from 101 or 901. The pattern is the same, and it makes numbers up to 1,000 friendly.

Mistakes to watch for

  • Writing 20 at the start of the third row instead of at the end of the second. Each row ends with a full ten.
  • Slips at the turn of a row, where 39 is followed by 40 on the same row and 41 starts the next.
  • Filling blanks only by counting from 1. Show the neighbour method; it is the whole point of the chart.
